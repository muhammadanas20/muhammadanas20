# Weekly plan

A week is the atomic unit of this course.

If you only have 30 minutes, you will thrash. If you have a weekly shape, you will finish.

---

## Default week (10–15 hours)

This matches the [180-day plan](./180_DAY_PLAN.md).

| Block | Hours | What |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Mon | 2 | Theory.md — read once, then re-read the diagrams |
| Tue | 2 | Examples.md — type every file, do the dry runs |
| Wed | 2 | Practice + beginner/medium exercises |
| Thu | 2 | Interview.md out loud + quiz |
| Fri | 2 | Mini-project or hard exercise |
| Sat | 2–3 | Finish the mini-project, write the README |
| Sun | 1 | Flashcards, progress tracker, rest |

Sunday is not optional. The tracker is how you notice you have been "busy" without shipping.

---

## Sprint week (20–25 hours)

This matches the [90-day plan](./90_DAY_PLAN.md).

Keep the same shape. Double the weekday blocks. Protect sleep. Tired engineers ship prompt injection.

---

## The four questions every Sunday

1. What can I **demo** that I could not demo last Sunday?
2. Which concept would I fail if a staff engineer asked me at a whiteboard?
3. Did I measure anything (latency, tokens, eval, dollars)?
4. What will I cut next week so I can go deeper?

If the answer to (1) is "I watched videos," the week did not count.

## When life happens

Missed three days? Do **not** "catch up" by skimming.

Repeat the last incomplete week. Depth beats a fake finish date.

Missed three weeks? Restart at the last phase whose exit ticket you can still demo.

---

## Pairing and study groups

If you study with someone:

- Split a debugging session, not the assignment
- Whiteboard RAG for each other on Thursdays
- Never share `.env`

Teaching a concept is the fastest way to find out you do not know it.
